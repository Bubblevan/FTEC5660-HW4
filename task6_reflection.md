# Task 6 Reflection

### What kind of person would I want to be?

I hope to use powerful agents as tools without treating their answers as conclusions. When a decision affects other people, I want to explain the evidence behind it, admit what I do not know, and take responsibility for the choice I make.

### Which ambitions would still matter?

My interest in the connection between finance and technology would still matter. Agents can write code and summarize reports, but someone still has to choose which problems deserve attention and what counts as a trustworthy result. I would like to help build tools that make decisions clearer and easier to check.

### What would I want to be known for beyond productivity and professional achievements?

I would want people to find me reliable: someone who tries to keep commitments, treats people fairly, and says when they do not know. These qualities show up in everyday choices, even when they do not appear in a work metric.

### What would I be willing to struggle for?

I would be willing to spend time understanding a problem, checking evidence, and tracing why an answer failed. In this public-set experiment, a basic retriever confused a company outside the corpus with a similarly named company and produced a specific amount. A comparison question also needed evidence for both companies before it could be answered reliably. Finding and correcting those errors took longer than accepting the first result, but it changed whether the answer could mislead someone.

### Which skills and qualities would still matter?

I want to keep practicing how to frame a question, judge whether the evidence is sufficient, understand financial and technical details, and explain uncertainty clearly. Listening and working with people matter too: a tool can help find information, while the people affected by a decision can explain what is at stake.

### If value were no longer defined mainly by output, how would I define myself?

I would look to the commitments I make, how I treat people around me, and what I choose when a convenient option feels wrong. The number of tasks I complete can say something about my abilities, but it cannot stand in for those things.

### What should I start doing differently tomorrow?

Before starting a task, I can write down the problem I am actually trying to solve and what evidence would support a good answer. I can give an agent a bounded task, check the sources behind important claims, and keep a short note of mistakes that should change my assumptions. For work that matters, I should leave enough time to review the reasoning instead of forwarding the generated result untouched.
